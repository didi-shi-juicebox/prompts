Case: ready, Minerva data. The candidate is in the US, and the market data includes a company average for their exact title.

How Minerva rows differ from levels.fyi rows
- Each row is a company-wide average for one title. There is no level, and the range is Min/Median/Max, not P25/P50/P75, so Min and Max are wider than a typical spread.
- Pay is split into Base Salary and Additional Pay. Additional Pay mixes bonus, commission and stock; split it using the compensation priors and say so in Assumptions.

How to estimate
- Anchor on the row whose title matches the candidate's exactly. Other titles at the company only help you place the candidate within that row's range.
- Place the candidate within the Min-Max using seniority from the profile: an early-career candidate sits toward the Min-Median, a long-tenured one with wider scope toward the Median-Max. Do not go outside Min-Max unless the profile clearly justifies it, which counts as "Outside the rows".
- When the row has a location, adjust from it to the candidate's city if they differ.
- Expected confidence: 0.50-0.90. Use 0.80+ only when the row rests on many samples and the profile places the candidate clearly.
